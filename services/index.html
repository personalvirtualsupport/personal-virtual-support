What I help with

Two halves of the same problem: the admin that never stops, and the tech that gets in the way. Most people need some of both.

Admin & organisation

Inbox kept under control, replies drafted, diary and appointments managed, files and paperwork tidied, and the day-to-day tasks that quietly stack up taken off your list.

Client follow-up

Chasing what is outstanding with clients and suppliers, confirming and reminding, and closing the loops that go quiet when everyone is busy.

Email marketing

Newsletters and campaigns in MailerLite, Mailchimp or Kit: templates built to match your brand, lists and segments tidied, signup forms connected, automations set up and tested before anything goes out.

Systems & automations

Trackers, forms and spreadsheets that people actually trust. And where a job is being done by hand thirty times a month, an automation in Make.com so it is done once, properly.

Websites & updates

New sites designed and built, and changes to the one you already have: WordPress, Elementor, page edits, new content, and the small fixes nobody else has got round to.

Tech troubleshooting

A form that stopped sending. A login nobody can find. Domains, DNS, email deliverability, Google Workspace, Zoom. Worked out and sorted, without you having to understand it first.

The jobs people actually bring me

Not everything fits a tidy service list. These are the kinds of things that turn up, and they are the work I like most.

“It used to work and now it doesn’t”

The enquiry form that quietly stopped sending. The newsletter that started landing in spam. Something changed, nobody knows what, and enquiries are going missing in the meantime.

“I started it and never finished”

The half-built mailing list. The website page still saying last year’s prices. The system you set up with good intentions in January. Picked up where you left off and finished.

“I do this every week and I hate it”

The same copying between two tools, the same reminder email, the same list rebuilt by hand. If it can be automated once instead of done thirty times, I will tell you, and then set it up.

“I don’t even know what to ask for”

You know something is not working, but not what it is called or who fixes it. Describe it in your own words. Working out what the job actually is comes as part of the job.
